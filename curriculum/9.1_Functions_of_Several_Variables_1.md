## 1. Two motivating examples (slide 2)

- The temperature $T$ at a point on the surface of the earth at any given time
  depends on the longitude $x$ and latitude $y$ of the point: $T=f(x,y)$.
- The volume $V$ of a circular cylinder depends on its radius $r$ and height
  $h$: $V=V(r,h)=\pi r^2h$.

## 2. Function of two variables (slides 3-4)

**Definition (slide 3).** A function $f$ of two variables is a rule that
assigns to each ordered pair of real numbers $(x,y)$ in a set $D$ a unique real
number denoted by $f(x,y)$:
$$z=f(x,y),\qquad (x,y)\in D$$
- $D$: the **domain** of $f$;
- $\{f(x,y)\mid(x,y)\in D\}$: the **range** of $f$;
- $x,y$: independent variables; $z$: dependent variable.

**Slide 4 (picture).** Compares $z=f(x,y)$, $(x,y)\in D$ with the notation
$y=f(x)$, $x\in D$ for one variable: $f$ sends each point $(x,y)$ of the plane
region $D$ to a number $f(x,y)$ on a number line (the $z$-axis), just as a
function of one variable sends each number $x$ to $f(x)$.

**Domain rule (slide 5).** If $f$ is given by a formula and no domain is
specified, then the domain of $f$ is understood to be the set of all pairs
$(x,y)$ for which the given expression is a well-defined real number.

## 3. Example 1: evaluate and find the domain (slides 5-8)

For each function, evaluate $f(3,2)$ and find the domain.
(a) $f(x,y)=\dfrac{\sqrt{x+y+1}}{x-1}$; (b) $f(x,y)=x\ln(y^2-x)$.

(a) $f(3,2)=\dfrac{\sqrt{3+2+1}}{3-1}=\dfrac{\sqrt6}{2}$. The expression makes
sense if the denominator is not $0$ and the quantity under the square root sign
is nonnegative. So the domain is
$$D=\{(x,y)\mid x+y+1\ge0,\ x\ne1\}$$
Slide 6 shows it as the half-plane on and above the line $x+y+1=0$, with the
vertical line $x=1$ removed.

(b) $f(3,2)=3\ln(4-3)=3\ln1=0$. Since $\ln(y^2-x)$ is defined only when
$y^2-x>0$, that is, $x<y^2$, the domain is
$$D=\{(x,y)\mid x<y^2\}$$
This is the set of points to the left of the parabola $x=y^2$ (slide 8 shows
the region with the parabola drawn dashed, i.e. not included).

## 4. Graph of a function of two variables (slides 9-11)

**Definition (slide 9).** If $f$ is a function of two variables with domain
$D$, then the **graph** of $f$ is the set of all points $(x,y,z)$ in $\mathbb R^3$
such that $z=f(x,y)$ and $(x,y)$ is in $D$. The graph is a surface $S$ with
equation $z=f(x,y)$; the point $(x,y,0)$ in $D$ lies directly below (or above)
the surface point $(x,y,f(x,y))$.

**Example 2 (slide 10).** Sketch the graph of $f(x,y)=6-3x-2y$. Slide note,
**Linear functions:** a function of the form $f(x,y)=Ax+By+C$. (No solution on
the slide; its graph is the plane $z=6-3x-2y$, as in 8.5.)

**Example 3 (slide 11).** (a) Sketch the graph of $f(x,y)=\sqrt{9-x^2-y^2}$.
(b) Find the domain and range and sketch the graph of $f(x,y)=4x^2+y^2$. (No
solution on the slide; compare 8.6: the first is the upper half of a sphere of
radius 3, the second an elliptic paraboloid.)

## 5. Three variables and $n$ variables (slides 12-13)

**Function of three variables (slide 12).** A rule that assigns to each
ordered triple $(x,y,z)$ in a set $D\subset\mathbb R^3$ a unique real number
$f(x,y,z)$:
$$u=f(x,y,z),\qquad (x,y,z)\in D$$
$D$ is the domain, $\{f(x,y,z)\mid(x,y,z)\in D\}$ the range; $x,y,z$ are the
independent variables, $u$ the dependent variable; the graph lies in
4-dimensional space.

**Function of $n$ variables (slide 13).** A rule that assigns a number
$z=f(x_1,x_2,\dots,x_n)$ to an $n$-tuple $(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ of real numbers.
$\mathbb R^n$ denotes the set of all such $n$-tuples:
$$u=f(x_1,x_2,\dots,x_n),\qquad(x_1,\dots,x_n)\in\mathbb R^n$$
with $\mathbb R^n$ (or the subset where $f$ is defined) the domain,
$x_1,\dots,x_n$ the independent variables and $u$ the dependent variable.
