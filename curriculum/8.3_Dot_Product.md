## 1. Dot Product of Two Vectors (slides 2-11)

**Definition (slide 2).** If $\vec a=\langle a_x,a_y,a_z\rangle$ and
$\vec b=\langle b_x,b_y,b_z\rangle$, the dot product (scalar product) of
$\vec a$ and $\vec b$ is the number
$$\vec a\cdot\vec b=a_xb_x+a_yb_y+a_zb_z$$
To find it, multiply corresponding components and add. The result is not a
vector: it is a real number, a scalar.

**Example 1 (slide 3).** $\vec a=\langle-1,7,4\rangle$,
$\vec b=\langle6,2,-\tfrac12\rangle$. Find $\vec a\cdot\vec b$.

**Example 2 (slide 3).** $\vec a=\vec i+2\vec j-3\vec k$, $\vec b=2\vec j-\vec k$.
Find $\vec a\cdot\vec b$. (No solutions on the slide for Examples 1-2.)

**Properties (slide 3).** For $\vec a=\langle a_x,a_y,a_z\rangle$,
$\vec b=\langle b_x,b_y,b_z\rangle$, and a scalar $c$ (the slide reuses the
letter $c$ for a vector in property 3, as printed):
1. $\vec a\cdot\vec a=|\vec a|^2=a_x^2+a_y^2+a_z^2$
2. $\vec a\cdot\vec b=\vec b\cdot\vec a$
3. $\vec a\cdot(\vec b+\vec c)=\vec a\cdot\vec b+\vec a\cdot\vec c$
4. $(c\vec a)\cdot\vec b=c(\vec a\cdot\vec b)=\vec a\cdot(c\vec b)$
5. $\vec 0\cdot\vec b=0$

**Geometric interpretation (slide 4).** By the Law of Cosines in the triangle
$OAB$ (with $\vec a=\vec{OA}$, $\vec b=\vec{OB}$):
$$|AB|^2=|OA|^2+|OB|^2-2|OA||OB|\cos\theta,\ \text{i.e.}\
|\vec a-\vec b|^2=|\vec a|^2+|\vec b|^2-2|\vec a||\vec b|\cos\theta$$
On the other hand
$|\vec a-\vec b|^2=(\vec a-\vec b)\cdot(\vec a-\vec b)=|\vec a|^2+|\vec b|^2-2\,\vec a\cdot\vec b$.
Comparing the two gives:

**Theorem (slide 4).** If $\theta$ is the angle between $\vec a$ and $\vec b$,
then
$$\vec a\cdot\vec b=|\vec a||\vec b|\cos\theta$$

**Example 3 (slide 5).** The vectors $\vec a$ and $\vec b$ have lengths 4 and 6,
and the angle between them is $\pi/3$. Find $\vec a\cdot\vec b$.

**Corollary (slide 5).** If $\theta$ is the angle between the nonzero vectors
$\vec a$ and $\vec b$, then
$$\cos\theta=\frac{\vec a\cdot\vec b}{|\vec a||\vec b|}$$

**Example 4 (slide 5).** Find the angle between $\vec a=\langle2,2,-1\rangle$
and $\vec b=\langle5,-3,2\rangle$. (No solution on the slide.)

**Orthogonal vectors (slide 6).** Nonzero $\vec a,\vec b$ are called
perpendicular or orthogonal if the angle between them is $\theta=\pi/2$. If
the angle is $\pi/2$, then
$\vec a\cdot\vec b=|\vec a||\vec b|\cos\frac\pi2=0$. Conversely, if
$\vec a\cdot\vec b=0$ with $|\vec a|\ne0$, $|\vec b|\ne0$, then $\cos\theta=0$,
so $\theta=\pi/2$.

**Theorem (slide 6).** Two vectors $\vec a$ and $\vec b$ are orthogonal if and
only if $\vec a\cdot\vec b=0$.

**Example 5 (slide 7).** Show that $2\vec i+2\vec j-\vec k$ is perpendicular to
$5\vec i-4\vec j+2\vec k$. Solution:
$(2\vec i+2\vec j-\vec k)\cdot(5\vec i-4\vec j+2\vec k)=2\times5+2\times(-4)+(-1)\times2=0$,
so the vectors are perpendicular.

**Sign of the dot product (slides 7-8).** From $\cos\theta=\dfrac{\vec a\cdot\vec b}{|\vec a||\vec b|}$:
- $0\le\theta<\frac\pi2\Rightarrow\cos\theta>0\Rightarrow\vec a\cdot\vec b>0$:
  $\vec a$ and $\vec b$ point in the same general direction.
- $\frac\pi2<\theta\le\pi\Rightarrow\cos\theta<0\Rightarrow\vec a\cdot\vec b<0$:
  they point in generally opposite directions.
- $\vec a\cdot\vec b=0$: perpendicular.

Slide 8 (Remark) draws three pairs of vectors labeled $\vec a\cdot\vec b>0$,
$\vec a\cdot\vec b>0$, $\vec a\cdot\vec b<0$. The middle pair is the
perpendicular one (right-angle mark), so its label is a slide typo and should
read $\vec a\cdot\vec b=0$.

**Direction angles and direction cosines (slides 9-10).** The direction angles
of a nonzero vector $\vec a$ are the angles $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ (in
$[0,\pi]$) that $\vec a$ makes with the positive $x$-, $y$-, $z$-axes. Their
cosines are the direction cosines of $\vec a$:
$$\cos\alpha=\frac{\vec a\cdot\vec i}{|\vec a||\vec i|}=\frac{a_x}{|\vec a|},\quad
\cos\beta=\frac{\vec a\cdot\vec j}{|\vec a||\vec j|}=\frac{a_y}{|\vec a|},\quad
\cos\gamma=\frac{\vec a\cdot\vec k}{|\vec a||\vec k|}=\frac{a_z}{|\vec a|}$$
$$\cos^2\alpha+\cos^2\beta+\cos^2\gamma=1,\qquad
\vec a=\langle|\vec a|\cos\alpha,\ |\vec a|\cos\beta,\ |\vec a|\cos\gamma\rangle$$
$$\Rightarrow\ \frac{1}{|\vec a|}\vec a=\langle\cos\alpha,\cos\beta,\cos\gamma\rangle$$
So the direction cosines of $\vec a$ are the components of the unit vector in
the direction of $\vec a$.

**Example 6 (slide 11).** Find the direction angles of $\vec a=\langle1,2,3\rangle$.
$|\vec a|=\sqrt{1^2+2^2+3^2}=\sqrt{14}$, so
$\cos\alpha=\frac1{\sqrt{14}}$, $\cos\beta=\frac2{\sqrt{14}}$,
$\cos\gamma=\frac3{\sqrt{14}}$, and
$\alpha=\cos^{-1}\frac1{\sqrt{14}}\approx74^\circ$,
$\beta=\cos^{-1}\frac2{\sqrt{14}}\approx58^\circ$,
$\gamma=\cos^{-1}\frac3{\sqrt{14}}\approx37^\circ$.

**Example 7 (slide 11).** (1) Find the direction cosines of the vector
$\vec a=\vec i-2\vec j+\vec k$. (2) Find the unit vector in the direction of
$\vec b=2\vec i-2\vec j+\vec k$. The slide prints the last term of both vectors
as $\vec z$; it clearly means $\vec k$. (No solution on the slide.)

## 2. Projections (slides 12-14)

**Definitions (slide 12).** Let $\vec a$ and $\vec b$ start at the same point
$P$, and let $S$ be the foot of the perpendicular dropped from the tip $R$ of
$\vec b$ onto the line of $\vec a$. The vector $\vec{PS}$ is the **vector
projection** of $\vec b$ onto $\vec a$, denoted $\operatorname{proj}_{\vec a}\vec b$.
The **scalar projection** of $\vec b$ onto $\vec a$ (also called the component
of $\vec b$ along $\vec a$) is the signed magnitude of the vector projection,
the number $|\vec b|\cos\theta$ (where $\theta$ is the angle between $\vec a$
and $\vec b$), denoted $\operatorname{comp}_{\vec a}\vec b$.

**Formulas (slide 13).**
$$\operatorname{comp}_{\vec a}\vec b=|\vec b|\cos\theta=\frac{\vec a\cdot\vec b}{|\vec a|}=\frac{\vec a}{|\vec a|}\cdot\vec b\qquad(\text{a number})$$
$$\operatorname{proj}_{\vec a}\vec b=\left(\operatorname{comp}_{\vec a}\vec b\right)\frac{\vec a}{|\vec a|}=\frac{\vec a\cdot\vec b}{|\vec a|^2}\,\vec a\qquad(\text{a vector})$$

**Example 8 (slide 14).** Find the scalar and vector projections of
$\vec b=\langle1,1,2\rangle$ onto $\vec a=\langle-2,3,1\rangle$.
$|\vec a|=\sqrt{(-2)^2+3^2+1^2}=\sqrt{14}$.
$$\operatorname{comp}_{\vec a}\vec b=\frac{\vec a\cdot\vec b}{|\vec a|}=\frac{(-2)\times1+3\times1+1\times2}{\sqrt{14}}=\frac{3}{\sqrt{14}}$$
$$\operatorname{proj}_{\vec a}\vec b=\frac{\vec a\cdot\vec b}{|\vec a|^2}\vec a=\frac{3}{14}\vec a=\left\langle-\frac37,\frac9{14},\frac3{14}\right\rangle$$
