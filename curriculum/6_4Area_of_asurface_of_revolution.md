## 1. What's a surface of revolution?

A surface of revolution is formed when a curve is rotated about a line
(examples: sphere, torus, and other surfaces formed this way).

## 2. What's the area of a surface of revolution?

If the surface area is $A$, painting it takes the same amount of paint as
a flat region of area $A$.

**Simple case (1): lateral surface of a circular cylinder**, radius $r$,
height $h$. Cut and unroll into a rectangle $h\times 2\pi r$:
$$A=2\pi rh$$

**Simple case (2): lateral surface of a circular cone**, base radius $r$,
slant height $l$. Cut and unroll into a sector of radius $l$ and angle
$\theta=\dfrac{2\pi r}{l}$:
$$A=\frac{l^2\theta}{2}=\pi rl$$

**Area of a band (frustum of a cone)**, radii $r_1$ (top) and $r_2$
(bottom), slant heights $l_1$ (to the apex) and $l$ (of the band):
$$A=\pi r_2(l_1+l)-\pi r_1 l_1=\pi[(r_2-r_1)l_1+r_2l]$$
Using similar triangles, $\dfrac{l_1}{r_1}=\dfrac{l_1+l}{r_2}$, this simplifies to
$$A=\pi(r_1l+r_2l)=2\pi rl,\qquad r=\frac{r_1+r_2}{2}\ (\text{average radius of the band})$$

## General case — same slicing/limit method as arc length

Divide $[a,b]$ into $n$ subintervals with endpoints $x_0,\dots,x_n$ and
width $\Delta x$ (as for arc length). Consider the part of the surface
between $x_{i-1}$ and $x_i$, approximated by rotating the segment
$\overline{P_{i-1}P_i}$ about the x-axis:
$$\Delta S_i \approx 2\pi\cdot\frac{y_{i-1}+y_i}{2}\cdot|P_{i-1}P_i|$$
Substituting $y_{i-1}=f(x_{i-1})$, $y_i=f(x_i)$, $|P_{i-1}P_i|=\sqrt{1+(f'(x_i^*))^2}\,\Delta x_i$:
$$\Delta S_i \approx 2\pi\,\frac{f(x_{i-1})+f(x_i)}{2}\sqrt{1+(f'(x_i^*))^2}\,\Delta x_i$$
For small $\Delta x_i$, $f(x_{i-1})\approx f(x_i)\approx f(x_i^*)$, so
$$\Delta S_i \approx 2\pi f(x_i^*)\sqrt{1+(f'(x_i^*))^2}\,\Delta x_i$$
Sum the bands, then take the limit:
$$S=\sum_{i=1}^n\Delta S_i \approx \sum_{i=1}^n 2\pi f(x_i^*)\sqrt{1+(f'(x_i^*))^2}\,\Delta x_i \quad\Rightarrow\quad S=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^n 2\pi f(x_i^*)\sqrt{1+(f'(x_i^*))^2}\,\Delta x_i=\int_a^b 2\pi f(x)\sqrt{1+(f'(x))^2}\,dx$$

**Definitions:**
- Rotating $y=f(x)$, $a\le x\le b$ ($f$ positive, continuously
  differentiable), about the **x-axis**:
  $$S=\int_a^b 2\pi f(x)\sqrt{1+(f'(x))^2}\,dx = \int 2\pi f(x)\,ds \qquad(\text{circumference}=2\pi y)$$
- Rotating $x=g(y)$, $c\le y\le d$ ($g$ positive, continuously
  differentiable), about the **y-axis**:
  $$S=\int_c^d 2\pi g(y)\sqrt{1+(g'(y))^2}\,dy = \int 2\pi g(y)\,ds \qquad(\text{circumference}=2\pi x)$$

**Remark (how to use it):** determine the curve's equation and which axis
it's rotated about; if about the x-axis, use $x$ as the independent
variable and find the interval (analogous for the y-axis); differentiate;
apply the formula.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Area of the surface obtained by rotating $y=\sqrt{4-x^2}$,
$-1\le x\le1$, about the x-axis (a portion of a sphere of radius 2).
$$y'=\frac{-x}{\sqrt{4-x^2}} \quad\Rightarrow\quad S=\int_{-1}^1 2\pi y\sqrt{1+(y')^2}\,dx = 2\pi\int_{-1}^1\sqrt{4-x^2}\sqrt{1+\frac{x^2}{4-x^2}}\,dx=2\pi\int_{-1}^1 2\,dx=8\pi$$

**Example 2.** Area of the surface obtained by rotating $y=x^2$,
$1\le x\le2$, about the **y-axis**. Independent variable is $y\in[1,4]$;
$x=\sqrt y$, $x'=\tfrac12y^{-1/2}$.
$$S=\int_1^4 2\pi x\sqrt{1+(x')^2}\,dy = 2\pi\int_1^4\sqrt y\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{4y}}\,dy=\pi\int_1^4\sqrt{4y+1}\,dy=\frac{\pi}{12}\left(17\sqrt{17}-5\sqrt5\right)$$

**Example 3.** Area of the surface generated by rotating $y=e^x$,
$0<x<1$, about the x-axis.
$$\frac{dy}{dx}=e^x \quad\Rightarrow\quad S=\int_0^1 2\pi y\sqrt{1+(dy/dx)^2}\,dx = 2\pi\int_0^1 e^x\sqrt{1+e^{2x}}\,dx$$
Substitute $u=e^x$, then $u=\tan\theta$:
$$=2\pi\int_1^e\sqrt{1+u^2}\,du = 2\pi\int_{\pi/4}^{\arctan e}\sec^3\theta\,d\theta = \pi\left[e\sqrt{1+e^2}+\ln(e+\sqrt{1+e^2})-\sqrt2-\ln(\sqrt2+1)\right]$$
