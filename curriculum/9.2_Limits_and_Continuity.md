## 1. Limits in the case of two variables (slides 2-4)

**Definition (slide 2).** Let $f$ be a function of two variables whose domain
$D$ includes points arbitrarily close to $(a,b)$. The values of $f(x,y)$
approach the number $L$ as the point $(x,y)$ approaches $(a,b)$ along **any path
that stays within the domain of $f$**. Then we say the limit of $f(x,y)$ as
$(x,y)$ approaches $(a,b)$ is $L$:
$$\lim_{(x,y)\to(a,b)}f(x,y)=L,\qquad(x,y)\in D$$
Slide notes: this limit is **not** the same as the iterated limit
$\lim_{x\to a}\lim_{y\to b}f(x,y)$. Key words: **along any path**.

**Slide 4 rule.** If different paths lead to different limiting values, the
given limit does not exist.

**Example 1 (slide 3).** Determine the limit of
$$f(x,y)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{xy}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}},&(x,y)\ne(0,0)\\[2mm]0,&(x,y)=(0,0)\end{cases}$$
as $(x,y)\to(0,0)$. The slide takes three paths:
- along the $x$-axis ($y=0$): $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{x\cdot0}{\sqrt{x^2+0^2}}=0$;
- along the $y$-axis ($x=0$): $\lim_{y\to0}\dfrac{0\cdot y}{\sqrt{0^2+y^2}}=0$;
- along $y=x$: $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{x\cdot x}{\sqrt{x^2+x^2}}$, which the slide
  writes as $=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$,

and concludes in a green box: "The limit does not exist".

**Slide error (checked by hand, confirmed against a zoomed render of the
slide).** The third path does not give $\frac{\sqrt2}2$: $\dfrac{x^2}{\sqrt{2x^2}}=\dfrac{x^2}{\sqrt2\,|x|}=\dfrac{|x|}{\sqrt2}\to0$. In fact the limit
exists and equals $0$: $|y|\le\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$ gives
$\left|\dfrac{xy}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}\right|\le|x|\to0$, so by squeezing the limit is
$0=f(0,0)$ and $f$ is continuous at the origin. The slide's example looks like
the textbook one for $\dfrac{xy}{x^2+y^2}$ (path $y=x$ gives $\frac12$, limit
does not exist) with the square root added by mistake. Teach the squeeze
argument, not the slide's conclusion, if this example comes up.

**Example 2 (slide 4).** Determine the limit of
$$f(x,y)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+y}},&(x,y)\ne(0,0)\\[2mm]0,&(x,y)=(0,0)\end{cases}$$
Along the $x$-axis ($y=0$): $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+0}}=\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{x}{|x|}=
\begin{cases}1,&x\to0^+\\-1,&x\to0^-\end{cases}$. Two different values, so the
limit does not exist.

## 2. Continuity (slides 5-9)

**Definition (slide 5).** A function $f$ of two variables is called continuous
at $(a,b)$ if
$$\lim_{(x,y)\to(a,b)}f(x,y)=f(a,b)$$
We say $f$ is continuous on $D$ if $f$ is continuous at every point $(a,b)$ in
$D$. Intuitive meaning: if the point $(x,y)$ changes by a small amount, then the
value of $f(x,y)$ changes by a small amount. So the graph of a continuous
function (a surface) has no hole or break.

**Polynomials and rational functions (slide 6).**
- A **polynomial function of two variables** is a sum of terms $cx^my^n$ with
  $c$ a constant and $m,n$ nonnegative integers. Slide example:
  $f(x,y)=2x^5-x^4y^2+6xy^4-7y+\frac16$.
- A **rational function** is a ratio of polynomials. Slide examples:
  $g(x,y)=\dfrac{3xy+2}{x^2+y^2}$, $h(x,y)=\dfrac{x^3y+3x+2}{x^2y+y^2}$.
- All polynomials are continuous on $\mathbb R^2$. Any rational function is
  continuous on its domain, because it is a quotient of continuous functions.

**Example 3 (slide 7).** Determine the limit:
(a) $\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(1,2)}(x^2y^3-x^3y^2+2x+3y)$;
(b) $\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)}\dfrac{5x^4+2xy-1}{x^2+2}$. (No solution
on the slide: both are continuous on their domains, so substitute the point.)

**Composition (slide 8).** If $f$ is a continuous function of two variables
and $g$ is a continuous function of a single variable defined on the range of
$f$, then the composite $h=g\circ f$, $h(x,y)=g(f(x,y))$, is also continuous.

**Example 4 (slide 8).** Where is $h(x,y)=\arctan\dfrac yx$ continuous? **Example
5 (slide 9).** Where is $g(x,y)=\dfrac{xy}{1+e^{x-y}}$ continuous? **Example 6
(slide 9).** Where is $F(x,y)=\cos\sqrt{1+x-y}$ continuous? (No solutions on the
slides. Domain hints: $x\ne0$ for Example 4; all of $\mathbb R^2$ for Example 5,
since $1+e^{x-y}>0$; $1+x-y\ge0$ for Example 6.)
