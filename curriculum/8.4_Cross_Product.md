## 1. Cross Product of Two Vectors (slides 2-6)

**Definition (slide 2).** If $\vec a=\langle a_x,a_y,a_z\rangle$ and
$\vec b=\langle b_x,b_y,b_z\rangle$, the cross product (vector product) is the
vector
$$\vec a\times\vec b=\langle a_yb_z-a_zb_y,\ a_zb_x-a_xb_z,\ a_xb_y-a_yb_x\rangle$$
The result is a vector, unlike the dot product. To make the definition easier
to remember, determinant notation is used.

**Determinants (slide 3).** Order 2:
$$\begin{vmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{vmatrix}=ad-bc,\qquad
\begin{vmatrix}1&-2\\6&-3\end{vmatrix}=1(-3)-6(-2)=9$$
Order 3 is defined through second-order determinants (expansion along the first
row):
$$\begin{vmatrix}a_1&a_2&a_3\\b_1&b_2&b_3\\c_1&c_2&c_3\end{vmatrix}=a_1\begin{vmatrix}b_2&b_3\\c_2&c_3\end{vmatrix}-a_2\begin{vmatrix}b_1&b_3\\c_1&c_3\end{vmatrix}+a_3\begin{vmatrix}b_1&b_2\\c_1&c_2\end{vmatrix}$$
Each term on the right involves a number $a_i$ of the first row; it is
multiplied by the second-order determinant obtained from the left side by
deleting the row and column in which it appears. Notice the minus sign in the
second term.

**Slide 4.** Example:
$$\begin{vmatrix}1&2&3\\0&-1&2\\-2&1&5\end{vmatrix}=1\begin{vmatrix}-1&2\\1&5\end{vmatrix}-2\begin{vmatrix}0&2\\-2&5\end{vmatrix}+3\begin{vmatrix}0&-1\\-2&1\end{vmatrix}=1(-5-2)-2(0-(-4))+3(0-2)$$
The slide ends this line with "$=-24$". That is an arithmetic slip: the line
gives $-7-8-6=-21$ (also $-21$ by the rule of Sarrus).

The cross product through a determinant, "the easiest way of remembering and
computing cross products":
$$\vec a\times\vec b=\begin{vmatrix}\vec i&\vec j&\vec k\\a_x&a_y&a_z\\b_x&b_y&b_z\end{vmatrix}=\begin{vmatrix}a_y&a_z\\b_y&b_z\end{vmatrix}\vec i-\begin{vmatrix}a_x&a_z\\b_x&b_z\end{vmatrix}\vec j+\begin{vmatrix}a_x&a_y\\b_x&b_y\end{vmatrix}\vec k$$

**Example 1 (slide 5).** $\vec a=\langle1,3,4\rangle$, $\vec b=\langle2,7,-5\rangle$.
$$\vec a\times\vec b=\begin{vmatrix}\vec i&\vec j&\vec k\\1&3&4\\2&7&-5\end{vmatrix}=(-15-28)\vec i-(-5-8)\vec j+(7-6)\vec k=-43\vec i+13\vec j+\vec k$$

**Example 2 (slide 6).** Show that $\vec a\times\vec a=\vec 0$ for any vector
$\vec a$ in $V_3$. Proof: the determinant with two equal rows
$(a_x,a_y,a_z)$ expands into three second-order determinants each equal to 0,
so $\vec a\times\vec a=0\vec i+0\vec j+0\vec k=\vec 0$.

## 2. Properties of the Cross Product (slides 7-16)

**Theorem (slide 7).** $\vec a\times\vec b$ is orthogonal to both $\vec a$ and
$\vec b$. Proof: with $\vec a\times\vec b$ as in the definition,
$$\vec a\cdot(\vec a\times\vec b)=a_x(a_yb_z-a_zb_y)+a_y(a_zb_x-a_xb_z)+a_z(a_xb_y-a_yb_x)=0$$
and similarly $\vec b\cdot(\vec a\times\vec b)=0$, so
$\vec a\perp(\vec a\times\vec b)$ and $\vec b\perp(\vec a\times\vec b)$.

**Right-hand rule (slide 8).** If the fingers of your right hand curl in the
direction of a rotation (through an angle less than $180^\circ$) from $\vec a$
to $\vec b$, then your thumb points in the direction of $\vec a\times\vec b$.

**Theorem (slides 9-10).** If $\theta$ is the angle between $\vec a$ and
$\vec b$ ($0\le\theta\le\pi$), then
$$|\vec a\times\vec b|=|\vec a||\vec b|\sin\theta$$
Proof: from the definitions,
$|\vec a\times\vec b|^2=(a_yb_z-a_zb_y)^2+(a_zb_x-a_xb_z)^2+(a_xb_y-a_yb_x)^2
=(a_x^2+a_y^2+a_z^2)(b_x^2+b_y^2+b_z^2)-(a_xb_x+a_yb_y+a_zb_z)^2
=|\vec a|^2|\vec b|^2-(\vec a\cdot\vec b)^2
=|\vec a|^2|\vec b|^2-|\vec a|^2|\vec b|^2\cos^2\theta
=|\vec a|^2|\vec b|^2\sin^2\theta$. Taking square roots, and using
$\sqrt{\sin^2\theta}=\sin\theta$ because $\sin\theta\ge0$ on $[0,\pi]$, gives
the formula. Thus $\vec a\times\vec b$ is the vector perpendicular to both
$\vec a$ and $\vec b$ whose orientation is given by the right-hand rule and
whose length is $|\vec a||\vec b|\sin\theta$.

**Corollary (slide 11).** Two nonzero vectors $\vec a,\vec b$ are parallel if
and only if $\vec a\times\vec b=\vec 0$ (parallel $\iff\theta=0$ or
$\theta=\pi\iff\sin\theta=0\iff|\vec a\times\vec b|=0$). The length of
$\vec a\times\vec b$ equals the area of the parallelogram determined by
$\vec a$ and $\vec b$: $A=|\vec a||\vec b|\sin\theta=|\vec a\times\vec b|$.

**Example 3 (slide 12).** Find a vector perpendicular to the plane through
$P(1,4,6)$, $Q(-2,5,-1)$, $R(1,-1,1)$. The vector $\vec{PQ}\times\vec{PR}$ is
perpendicular to both $\vec{PQ}$ and $\vec{PR}$, hence to the plane.
$\vec{PQ}=\langle-3,1,-7\rangle$, $\vec{PR}=\langle0,-5,-5\rangle$,
$$\vec{PQ}\times\vec{PR}=\begin{vmatrix}\vec i&\vec j&\vec k\\-3&1&-7\\0&-5&-5\end{vmatrix}=-40\vec i-15\vec j+15\vec k$$
So $\langle-40,-15,15\rangle$ is perpendicular to the plane; any nonzero
scalar multiple, such as $\langle-8,-3,3\rangle$, is too.

**Example 4 (slide 13).** Find $\vec a\times\vec b$: (1) $\vec a=\langle1,-1,2\rangle$,
$\vec b=\langle-2,0,3\rangle$; (2) $\vec a=\langle1,0,0\rangle$,
$\vec b=\langle0,1,0\rangle$; (3) $\vec a=\langle-2,\sqrt2,1\rangle$,
$\vec b=\langle1,5,-3\rangle$. (No solutions on the slide.)

**Example 5 (slide 14).** Find the area of the triangle with vertices
$P(1,4,6)$, $Q(-2,5,-1)$, $R(1,-1,1)$. With $\vec{PQ}\times\vec{PR}=-40\vec i-15\vec j+15\vec k$
from Example 3, the parallelogram with adjacent sides $\vec{PQ},\vec{PR}$ has
area $|\vec{PQ}\times\vec{PR}|=\sqrt{(-40)^2+(-15)^2+15^2}$, and the triangle is
half of it: $A=\frac52\sqrt{82}$.

**Basis vectors (slide 15).** $\vec i\times\vec j=\vec k$,
$\vec j\times\vec k=\vec i$, $\vec k\times\vec i=\vec j$, and
$\vec j\times\vec i=-\vec k$, $\vec k\times\vec j=-\vec i$,
$\vec i\times\vec k=-\vec j$. So $\vec i\times\vec j\ne\vec j\times\vec i$: the
cross product is not commutative. In general $\vec a\times\vec b=-\vec b\times\vec a$.

**Theorem (slide 16).** For vectors $\vec a,\vec b,\vec c$ and a scalar $k$:
1. $\vec a\times\vec b=-\vec b\times\vec a$
2. $(k\vec a)\times\vec b=k(\vec a\times\vec b)=\vec a\times(k\vec b)$
3. $\vec a\times(\vec b+\vec c)=\vec a\times\vec b+\vec a\times\vec c$
4. $(\vec a+\vec b)\times\vec c=\vec a\times\vec c+\vec b\times\vec c$
5. $\vec a\cdot(\vec b\times\vec c)=(\vec a\times\vec b)\cdot\vec c$
6. $\vec a\times(\vec b\times\vec c)=(\vec a\cdot\vec c)\vec b-(\vec a\cdot\vec b)\vec c$

Scalar triple product (slide 16):
$$\vec a\cdot(\vec b\times\vec c)=\begin{vmatrix}a_x&a_y&a_z\\b_x&b_y&b_z\\c_x&c_y&c_z\end{vmatrix}$$

### Exercises and worked solutions

**Exercises (slide 17).**
(a) Find the cross product $\vec a\times\vec b$ and verify that it is orthogonal
to both $\vec a$ and $\vec b$: (1) $\vec a=\langle6,0,-2\rangle$,
$\vec b=\langle0,8,0\rangle$; (2) $\vec a=t\vec i+\cos t\,\vec j+\sin t\,\vec k$,
$\vec b=\vec i-\sin t\,\vec j+\cos t\,\vec k$.
(b) Find two vectors orthogonal to both $\langle3,2,1\rangle$ and $\langle-1,1,0\rangle$.
(c) Find a nonzero vector orthogonal to the plane through the points $P,Q,R$,
and find the area of triangle $PQR$: (1) $P(-1,3,1)$, $Q(0,5,2)$, $R(4,3,-1)$;
(2) $P(3,0,1)$, $Q(-1,2,5)$, $R(0,4,2)$.

**Solutions shown on slides 18-21** (these are the deck's own answers):
- (a)(1): $\vec a\times\vec b=16\vec i+0\vec j+48\vec k=\langle16,0,48\rangle$;
  $\vec a\cdot(\vec a\times\vec b)=0$ and $\vec b\cdot(\vec a\times\vec b)=0$.
- (a)(2): $\vec a\times\vec b=\vec i-(t\cos t-\sin t)\vec j+(\dots)\vec k$. The
  slide prints the $\vec k$-component as $-t\cos t-\sin t$, which is a typo; the
  determinant gives $t(-\sin t)-\cos t\cdot1=-t\sin t-\cos t$, so
  $\vec a\times\vec b=\langle1,\ -t\cos t+\sin t,\ -t\sin t-\cos t\rangle$ (with
  the typo, the dot product with $\vec a$ would not be 0).
- (b): $\langle3,2,1\rangle\times\langle-1,1,0\rangle=-\vec i-\vec j+5\vec k=\langle-1,-1,5\rangle$;
  choose $\vec a=\langle-1,-1,5\rangle$, $\vec b=-2\vec a=\langle2,2,-10\rangle$.
- (c)(1): $\vec{PQ}=\langle1,2,1\rangle$, $\vec{PR}=\langle5,0,-2\rangle$,
  $\vec{PQ}\times\vec{PR}=-4\vec i+7\vec j-10\vec k$; this is perpendicular to the
  plane; area of the parallelogram $\sqrt{16+49+100}=\sqrt{165}$, triangle
  $A=\frac12\sqrt{165}$. (The slide shows no solution for (2).)
