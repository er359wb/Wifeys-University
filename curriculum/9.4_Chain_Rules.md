## 1. Recall the one-variable chain rule (slide 2)

If $y=f(u)$ and $u=g(x)$ are differentiable, then $y$ is indirectly a
differentiable function of $x$ and
$$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{dy}{du}\cdot\frac{du}{dx}$$
A partial derivative is exactly an ordinary derivative with the other variables
regarded as constants, so the chain rule **does work** for functions of more
than one variable.

## 2. Case 1: one independent variable (slides 3-6)

**The Chain Rule (Case 1), slides 3-4.** Suppose $z=f(u,v)$, $u=g(t)$ and
$v=h(t)$ are all differentiable. Then $z$ is a differentiable function of $t$
and
$$\frac{dz}{dt}=\frac{\partial z}{\partial u}\cdot\frac{du}{dt}+\frac{\partial z}{\partial v}\cdot\frac{dv}{dt}$$
**Proof sketch (slides 3-4).** A change $\Delta t$ in $t$ produces changes
$\Delta u$ in $u$ and $\Delta v$ in $v$. Since $f$ is differentiable,
$\Delta z=\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial u}\Delta u+\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial v}\Delta v+\varepsilon_1\Delta u+\varepsilon_2\Delta v$ where
$\varepsilon_1\to0$ and $\varepsilon_2\to0$ as $(\Delta u,\Delta v)\to(0,0)$.
Dividing by $\Delta t$:
$\dfrac{\Delta z}{\Delta t}=\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial u}\dfrac{\Delta u}{\Delta t}+\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial v}\dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}+\varepsilon_1\dfrac{\Delta u}{\Delta t}+\varepsilon_2\dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$.
Letting $\Delta t\to0$ the last two terms go to $0$ and the formula follows.

**Example 1 (slide 5).** If $z=u^2v+3uv^4$, where $u=\sin2t$ and $v=\cos t$,
find $\dfrac{dz}{dt}$ when $t=0$. The Chain Rule gives
$$\frac{dz}{dt}=\frac{\partial z}{\partial u}\frac{du}{dt}+\frac{\partial z}{\partial v}\frac{dv}{dt}=(2uv+3v^4)\,2\cos2t+(u^2+12uv^3)(-\sin t)$$
When $t=0$: $u(0)=0$, $v(0)=1$. So
$\dfrac{dz}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}=(0+3)(2\cos0)+(0+0)(-\sin0)=6$.

**Example 2 (slide 6).** The pressure $P$ (kilopascals), volume $V$ (liters) and
temperature $T$ (kelvins) of a mole of ideal gas are related by $PV=8.31\,T$.
Find the rate at which the pressure is changing when the temperature is $300$ K
and increasing at $0.1$ K/s and the volume is $100$ L and increasing at
$0.2$ L/s. With $t$ in seconds: $T=300$, $\dfrac{dT}{dt}=0.1$, $V=100$,
$\dfrac{dV}{dt}=0.2$, and $P=8.31\dfrac TV$. So
$$\frac{dP}{dt}=\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\frac{dT}{dt}+\frac{\partial P}{\partial V}\frac{dV}{dt}=\frac{8.31}{V}\frac{dT}{dt}-\frac{8.31\,T}{V^2}\frac{dV}{dt}=-0.04155$$
(kPa per second; $0.00831-0.04986=-0.04155$.)

## 3. Case 2 and the general version (slides 7-9)

**The Chain Rule (Case 2), slides 7-8.** Suppose $z=f(u,v)$ is differentiable
and $u=g(x,y)$, $v=h(x,y)$ are both differentiable functions of $x$ and $y$.
Then
$$\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}=\frac{\partial z}{\partial u}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial z}{\partial v}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x},\qquad
\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}=\frac{\partial z}{\partial u}\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}+\frac{\partial z}{\partial v}\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}$$
$x,y$ are the **independent** variables, $u,v$ the **intermediate** variables,
$z$ the **dependent** variable. **Tree diagram:** draw $z$ at the top, branches to
$u$ and $v$, and from each of those branches to $x$ and $y$, labelling each
branch with the partial derivative along it. To find $\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial x}$, take the product of the partial derivatives along each path from
$z$ to $x$ and then add these products; similarly for $\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial y}$.
With $n$ intermediate variables $u_1,\dots,u_n$ each a function of $m$
variables $x_1,\dots,x_m$, use the same method.

**The Chain Rule (General Version), slide 9.** Suppose $z=f(u_1,\dots,u_n)$
is differentiable and each $u_j$ is a differentiable function of the $m$
variables $x_1,\dots,x_m$. Then $z$ is a function of $x_1,\dots,x_m$ and
$$\frac{\partial z}{\partial x_i}=\frac{\partial z}{\partial u_1}\frac{\partial u_1}{\partial x_i}+\frac{\partial z}{\partial u_2}\frac{\partial u_2}{\partial x_i}+\cdots+\frac{\partial z}{\partial u_n}\frac{\partial u_n}{\partial x_i}\qquad\text{for each }i=1,\dots,m$$

## 4. Examples 3-6 (slides 10-13)

**Example 3 (slide 10).** Write out the Chain Rule for $s=f(u,v,w)$ with
$u=u(x,y,z)$, $v=v(x,y,z)$, $w=w(x,y,z)$ (tree diagram: $s$ to $u,v,w$, each to
$x,y,z$):
$$\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}=\frac{\partial s}{\partial u}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial s}{\partial v}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial s}{\partial w}\frac{\partial w}{\partial x}$$
and the same with $y$ and with $z$ in place of $x$.

**Example 4 (slide 11).** If $s=u^4v+v^2w^2$, where $u=xye^z$, $v=xy^2e^{-z}$,
$w=x^2y\sin z$, find $\dfrac{\partial s}{\partial x}$ when $x=2,y=1,z=0$.
$$\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}=4u^3v\cdot ye^z+(u^4+2vw^2)\cdot y^2e^{-z}+2v^2w\cdot2xy\sin z$$
When $x=2,y=1,z=0$: $u=2$, $v=2$, $w=0$. Thus
$\dfrac{\partial s}{\partial x}=4\cdot2^3\cdot2+2^4\cdot1+0=80$.

**Example 5 (slide 12).** If $z=u\ln v+xu$, where $u=\sin x^2y$ and $v=\cos xy^2$,
find $\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial x}$. Here $z$ depends on $x$ both through $u,v$
and directly (the tree diagram has a third branch $z\to x$). The slide
highlights that the left $\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial x}$ and the last term's
$\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x}$ are different things (total effect vs the
partial derivative with $u,v$ held fixed):
$$\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial v}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\frac{dx}{dx}
=(\ln v+x)\,2xy\cos x^2y+\frac uv\,(-y^2\sin xy^2)+u$$
$$=(\ln\cos xy^2+x)\,2xy\cos x^2y+\frac{\sin x^2y}{\cos xy^2}(-y^2\sin xy^2)+\sin x^2y$$

**Example 6 (slide 13).** If $s=f(x+y+z,\,xyz)$, find $\dfrac{\partial^2s}{\partial x\,\partial z}$.
Let $u=x+y+z$, $v=xyz$, $s=f(u,v)$. Then
$\dfrac{\partial s}{\partial x}=\dfrac{\partial s}{\partial u}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x}+\dfrac{\partial s}{\partial v}\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial x}=\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial u}+\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial v}yz=f_1'+f_2'\,yz$.
Differentiating $f_1'$ and $f_2'$ again with respect to $z$ (they are functions of
$u,v$): $\dfrac{\partial f_1'}{\partial z}=f_{11}''+f_{12}''\,xy$,
$\dfrac{\partial f_2'}{\partial z}=f_{21}''+f_{22}''\,xy$. So
$$\frac{\partial^2s}{\partial x\,\partial z}=\frac{\partial(f_1'+f_2'yz)}{\partial z}=f_{11}''+f_{12}''xy+y\big[f_2'+z\,(f_{21}''+f_{22}''xy)\big]$$

## 5. Implicit differentiation (slides 15-21)

**Setup (slide 15).** Suppose an equation of the form $F(x,y)=0$ defines $y$
implicitly as a differentiable function of $x$: $y=f(x)$, where $F(x,f(x))=0$
for all $x$ in the domain of $f$. Differentiating both sides with the Chain Rule
(Case 1), with the tree $F\to x\to x$ and $F\to y\to x$:
$$\frac{\partial F}{\partial x}\frac{dx}{dx}+\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}\frac{dy}{dx}=0$$
**Slide 16.** If $\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial y}\ne0$, then (using $dx/dx=1$)
$$\frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{\partial F/\partial x}{\partial F/\partial y}=-\frac{F_x}{F_y}$$

**The Implicit Theorem (slide 17).** Suppose $F$ is defined on a disk
containing $(a,b)$, where $F(a,b)=0$, $F_y(a,b)\ne0$, and $F_x$ and $F_y$ are
continuous on the disk. Then the equation $F(x,y)=0$ defines $y$ as a function
of $x$ near the point $(a,b)$ and the derivative of this function is
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-\dfrac{F_x}{F_y}$.

**Example 7 (slide 18).** Find $y'$ if $x^3+y^3=6xy$. Write it as
$F(x,y)=x^3+y^3-6xy=0$. By the Implicit Theorem $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-\dfrac{F_x}{F_y}$
with $F_x=3x^2-6y$, $F_y=3y^2-6x$, so
$$\frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{3x^2-6y}{3y^2-6x}$$

**Two-variable version (slides 19-20).** Suppose $z$ is given implicitly as a
function $z=f(x,y)$ by an equation of the form $F(x,y,z)=0$, so
$F(x,y,f(x,y))=0$. If $f$ and $F$ are differentiable, the Chain Rule gives
$\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial x}\dfrac{dx}{dx}+\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial y}\dfrac{dy}{dx}+\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial z}\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial x}=0$ with $\dfrac{dx}{dx}=1$ and $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=0$. If $\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial z}\ne0$:
$$\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}=-\frac{F_x}{F_z},\qquad\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}=-\frac{F_y}{F_z}$$

**Example 8 (slide 21).** Find $\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial x}$ and $\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial y}$ if
$x^3+y^3+z^3+6xyz=1$ (the slide prints $\partial z/\partial x$ twice by mistake;
it means $\partial z/\partial y$ the second time). Write
$F(x,y,z)=x^3+y^3+z^3+6xyz-1=0$, so $F_x=3x^2+6yz$, $F_y=3y^2+6xz$,
$F_z=3z^2+6xy$:
$$\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}=-\frac{F_x}{F_z}=-\frac{3x^2+6yz}{3z^2+6xy}=-\frac{x^2+2yz}{z^2+2xy},\qquad
\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}=-\frac{F_y}{F_z}=-\frac{y^2+2xz}{z^2+2xy}$$
