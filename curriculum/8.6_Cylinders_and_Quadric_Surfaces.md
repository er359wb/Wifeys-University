## 1. Cylinders (slides 2-7)

**Traces (slide 2).** To sketch the graph of a surface, find the curves where
the surface meets planes parallel to the coordinate planes. These curves are
called *traces* (or cross-sections) of the surface.

**Cylinder (slide 2).** A surface consisting of all lines (called *rulings*)
that are parallel to a given line and pass through a given plane curve.

**Example 1 (slide 3).** Sketch $z=x^2$. The equation does not involve $y$, so
every vertical plane $y=k$ (parallel to the $xz$-plane) meets the graph in the
curve $z=x^2$. The vertical traces are parabolas. The graph is the parabola
$z=x^2$ in the $xz$-plane moved in the direction of the $y$-axis; the rulings
are parallel to the $y$-axis. This is a *parabolic cylinder*.

**Rule (slide 4).** If one of the variables $x,y,z$ is missing from the
equation of a surface, the surface is a cylinder. Its rulings are parallel to
the axis of the missing variable, and its cross-sections are the same curve
(given by the equation).

**Example 2 (slides 5-6).** Identify and sketch: (a) $x^2+y^2=1$: $z$ is
missing, the rulings are parallel to the $z$-axis, the cross-section in the
plane $z=k$ is a circle of radius 1. (b) $z^2+y^2=1$: $x$ is missing, the
rulings are parallel to the $x$-axis, the cross-section in the plane $x=k$ is a
circle of radius 1.

**Remark (slide 7).** An equation like $x^2+y^2=1$ is a circle in 2-D but a
cylinder in 3-D. To describe a circle in 3-D, give it as the intersection of
two surfaces, e.g. the trace of the cylinder $x^2+y^2=1$ in the $xy$-plane:
$x^2+y^2=1,\ z=0$. Exercises on slide 7: identify and sketch (a) $y=\frac1x$,
(b) $y^2-z^2=1$.

## 2. Quadric surfaces (slides 8-18)

**Definition (slide 8).** A quadric surface is the graph of a second-degree
equation in three variables $x,y,z$; the most general one is
$$Ax^2+By^2+Cz^2+Dxy+Eyz+Fxz+Gx+Hy+Iz+J=0$$
with constants $A,B,\dots,J$.

**Example 3 (slide 9).** Sketch $z=y^2-x^2$. The traces in the vertical planes
$x=k$ are the parabolas $z=y^2-k^2$; in the planes $y=k$ the parabolas
$z=-x^2+k^2$; the horizontal traces are $y^2-x^2=k$, a family of hyperbolas
(this is a hyperbolic paraboloid; the slide then shows the traces placed in
their correct planes).

**Example 4 (slide 10).** Sketch $\dfrac{x^2}{4}+y^2-\dfrac{z^2}{4}=1$. The trace
in any horizontal plane $z=k$ is the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{4}+y^2=1+\dfrac{k^2}{4}$.
The traces in the $xz$-plane and $yz$-plane are the hyperbolas
$\dfrac{x^2}{4}-\dfrac{z^2}{4}=1$ ($y=0$) and $y^2-\dfrac{z^2}{4}=1$ ($x=0$).
This surface is called a **hyperboloid of one sheet**.

**Table of standard quadric surfaces (slides 11-16)** (with $a,b,c$ constants):

| Surface | Equation | Traces and remarks |
|---|---|---|
| Ellipsoid (slide 11) | $\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}+\frac{z^2}{c^2}=1$ | All traces are ellipses. If $a=b=c$ it is a sphere. |
| Cone (slide 12) | $\frac{z^2}{c^2}=\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}$ | Horizontal traces are ellipses. Vertical traces in the planes $x=k$ and $y=k$ are hyperbolas if $k\ne0$ but pairs of lines if $k=0$. |
| Elliptic paraboloid (slide 13) | $\frac zc=\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}$ | Horizontal traces are ellipses; vertical traces are parabolas. The variable raised to the first power indicates the axis of the paraboloid. |
| Hyperboloid of one sheet (slide 14) | $\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}-\frac{z^2}{c^2}=1$ | Horizontal traces are ellipses; vertical traces are hyperbolas. The axis of symmetry corresponds to the variable whose coefficient is negative. |
| Hyperbolic paraboloid (slide 15) | $\frac zc=\frac{x^2}{a^2}-\frac{y^2}{b^2}$ | Horizontal traces are hyperbolas; vertical traces are parabolas. The case $c<0$ is illustrated. |
| Hyperboloid of two sheets (slide 16) | $-\frac{x^2}{a^2}-\frac{y^2}{b^2}+\frac{z^2}{c^2}=1$ | Horizontal traces in $z=k$ are ellipses if $k>c$ or $k<-c$; vertical traces are hyperbolas. The two minus signs indicate two sheets. |

**Example 5 (slide 17).** Identify and sketch $4x^2-y^2+2z^2+4=0$. The slide
shows only the picture (two separate bowls along the $y$-axis with vertices
$(0,\pm2,0)$, i.e. a hyperboloid of two sheets with axis the $y$-axis). No
solution text.

**Example 6 (slide 18).** Classify the quadric surface
$x^2+2z^2-6x-y+10=0$. Completing the square gives
$$y-1=(x-3)^2+2z^2$$
Comparing with the table, this is an elliptic paraboloid; here the axis of the
paraboloid is parallel to the $y$-axis, and it is shifted so that its vertex is
$(3,1,0)$. The traces in the planes $y=k$ ($k>1$) are the ellipses
$(x-3)^2+2z^2=k-1,\ y=k$. The trace in the $xy$-plane is the parabola
$y=1+(x-3)^2,\ z=0$.

**Exercises (slide 19).**
1. Describe and sketch the surface $x^2+z^2=1$.
2. Reduce the equation to one of the standard forms (if necessary), and use
traces to sketch and identify the surface: (1) $z=x^2+y^2$;
(2) $x^2=y^2+4z^2$; (3) $-x^2+4y^2-z^2=4$; (4) $y=z^2-x^2$;
(5) $4x^2+y^2+4z^2-4y-24z+36=0$; (6) $x^2-y^2+z^2-2x+2y+4z+2=0$.

**Exercises (slide 20).** Match the equation with its graph (labeled I-VIII;
the eight graphs are pictures: cones, hyperboloids, an ellipsoid, paraboloids,
a cylinder). Give reasons for your choice: (1) $x^2+4y^2+9z^2=1$;
(2) $9x^2+4y^2+z^2=1$; (3) $y=2x^2+z^2$; (4) $y^2=x^2+2z^2$;
(5) $x^2-y^2+z^2=1$; (6) $-x^2+y^2-z^2=1$; (7) $x^2+2z^2=1$; (8) $y=x^2-z^2$.
