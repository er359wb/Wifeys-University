## 1. Modeling with a differential equation (slides 2-6)

**Population growth (slides 2-3).** Assumption: the population grows at a rate
proportional to its size. Reasonable for bacteria or animals under ideal
conditions: unlimited environment, adequate nutrition, absence of predators,
immunity from disease. $t$ = time (independent variable), $P$ = number of
individuals (dependent variable), $\dfrac{dP}{dt}$ = the rate of growth:
$$\frac{dP}{dt}=kP$$
where $k$ is the proportionality constant. This is the first model of
population growth and it is a differential equation because it contains an
unknown function $P(t)$ and its derivative.

**Solving it (slide 4).** We look for a function whose derivative is a constant
multiple of itself: $P'(t)=kP(t)$. If $P(t)>0$ and $k>0$ for all $t$, then
$P'(t)>0$, so the population is always increasing. Try $P(t)=Ce^{kt}$: then
$P'(t)=Cke^{kt}=kP(t)$, so $P(t)=Ce^{kt}$ solves it. This is the general
solution of $\dfrac{dP}{dt}=kP$.

**Family of solutions (slide 5).** The graphs of $P=Ce^{kt}$ for different
values of $C$ form a family of curves (for $C>0$ and $t\ge0$ the curves grow
exponentially).

**Initial condition (slide 6).** $C$ turns out to be the initial population
$P(0)$. If $P(0)=1000$ then $C=1000$ and $P(t)=1000e^{kt}$: the solution of the
initial-value problem
$$\frac{dP}{dt}=kP(t),\qquad P(0)=1000$$

## 2. General differential equations (slides 7-12)

**Differential equation (slide 7).** An equation that contains an unknown
function and one or more of its derivatives. Examples: $\dfrac{dP(t)}{dt}=kP(t)$,
$f''(x)+f'(x)=0$, $r^{(20)}(\theta)=e^{2\theta}$.

**Order (slide 7).** The order of a differential equation is the order of the
highest derivative that occurs in the equation.

**Solution (slide 8).** A function $f$ is a solution of a differential equation
if the equation is satisfied when $y=f(x)$ and its derivatives are substituted
into the equation. Slide 8 shows the solutions: $P(t)=Ce^{kt}$;
$f(x)=C_1e^{-x}$ (for $f''+f'=0$);
$r(\theta)=\dfrac1{2^{20}}e^{2\theta}+C_1x^{19}+C_2x^{18}$ (for
$r^{(20)}=e^{2\theta}$).

**General solution (slide 9).** The solution that includes the same number of
arbitrary constants as the order of the equation:
- $\dfrac{dP}{dt}=kP$ (order 1): $P(t)=Ce^{kt}$;
- $f''+f'=0$ (order 2): $f(x)=C_1e^{-x}+C_2$;
- $r^{(20)}(\theta)=e^{2\theta}$ (order 20):
$r(\theta)=\dfrac1{2^{20}}e^{2\theta}+C_1x^{19}+C_2x^{18}+\dots+C_{19}x+C_{20}$.
(The slide writes the polynomial part with $x$ although the variable of $r$ is
$\theta$; it means powers of $\theta$.)

**Particular solution (slide 10).** A solution without any arbitrary constants:
$P(t)=1000e^{kt}$; $f(x)=e^{-x}+1$; $r(\theta)=\dfrac1{2^{20}}e^{2\theta}-1$.

**Initial condition and initial-value problem (slide 11).** A condition of the
form $y(t_0)=y_0$ is an initial condition. The problem of finding a solution
of the differential equation that satisfies the initial condition is an
initial-value problem. Examples on the slide: $\dfrac{dP}{dt}=kP(t),\ P(0)=1000$
and $f''(x)+f'(x)=0,\ f(0)=0,\ f'(0)=1$.

**Interpretations (slide 12).** Geometrical: among the family of solution
curves, pick the one that passes through the point $(t_0,y(t_0))$. Physical:
measure the state of a system at time $t_0$ and use the solution of the
initial-value problem to predict the future behavior of the system.

**Example 1 (slide 13).** Match the differential equations with the solution
graphs labeled I-IV (give reasons): (a) $y'=1+x^2+y^2$; (b) $y'=xe^{-x^2-y^2}$;
(c) $y'=\dfrac{1}{1+e^{x^2+y^2}}$; (d) $y'=\sin(xy)\cos(xy)$. The slide shows
the answer under the graphs: I is (b), II is (d), III is (a), IV is (c).

**Example 2 (slide 14).** Which of the following functions are solutions of
$y''+y=\sin x$? (a) $y=\sin x$; (b) $y=\cos x$; (c) $y=\frac12x\sin x$;
(d) $y=-\frac12x\cos x$. (No solution on the slide.)

**Example 3 (slide 14).** What is the order of: (a) $y'''-y+x^4=0$;
(b) $(y'')^3-xy^5=1$; (c) $y'+e^x\sin x=y^2$? (No solution on the slide.)

**Example 4 (slide 14).** Verify that $y=-t\cos t-t$ is a solution of the
initial-value problem $t\dfrac{dy}{dt}=y+t^2\sin t,\ y(\pi)=0$. (No solution on
the slide.)
