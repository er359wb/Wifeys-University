## 1. Second-order linear equations and superposition (slides 2-4)

**Form (slide 2).** A second-order linear differential equation has the form
$$P(x)\,y''+Q(x)\,y'+R(x)\,y=G(x)$$
where $P,Q,R,G$ are continuous functions. If $G(x)=0$ for all $x$ the equation is
**homogeneous**; otherwise it is **nonhomogeneous** (7.5). This section handles
the homogeneous case $P y''+Q y'+R y=0$.

**Superposition (slide 3).** If $y_1(x)$ and $y_2(x)$ are both solutions of
the linear homogeneous equation $P y''+Q y'+R y=0$ and $c_1,c_2$ are constants,
then $y=c_1y_1+c_2y_2$ is also a solution. Proof: substitute
$y=c_1y_1+c_2y_2$ into $Py''+Qy'+Ry$; by linearity of the derivative it equals
$c_1(Py_1''+Qy_1'+Ry_1)+c_2(Py_2''+Qy_2'+Ry_2)=c_1\cdot0+c_2\cdot0=0$.

**General solution (slide 4).** Two solutions $y_1,y_2$ are **linearly
independent** if neither is a constant multiple of the other (otherwise they are
linearly dependent). If $y_1,y_2$ are linearly independent solutions of the
homogeneous equation on an interval, then the general solution is
$$y=c_1y_1(x)+c_2y_2(x)$$
with $c_1,c_2$ arbitrary constants (two constants for order two, as in 7.1).

## 2. Constant coefficients: the characteristic equation (slides 5-8)

**Equation (slide 5).** Consider $a y''+b y'+c y=0$ with constants $a,b,c$
($a\ne0$). Try $y=e^{rx}$: then $y'=re^{rx}$, $y''=r^2e^{rx}$, and substituting
gives $(ar^2+br+c)e^{rx}=0$. Since $e^{rx}\ne0$, $y=e^{rx}$ is a solution
exactly when $r$ satisfies the **characteristic equation**
$$ar^2+br+c=0$$
with roots $r_{1,2}=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}$. What the general solution
looks like depends on the sign of $b^2-4ac$.

**Case 1: two distinct real roots $r_1\ne r_2$ (slide 6).** $e^{r_1x}$ and
$e^{r_2x}$ are linearly independent, so
$$y=C_1e^{r_1x}+C_2e^{r_2x}$$

**Case 2: one repeated real root $r_1=r_2=r$ (slide 6).** $e^{rx}$ is one
solution; a second, linearly independent one is $xe^{rx}$. So
$$y=C_1e^{rx}+C_2xe^{rx}$$

**Case 3: complex roots $r=\alpha\pm i\beta$ (slide 7).** Writing the solutions
$e^{(\alpha\pm i\beta)x}=e^{\alpha x}(\cos\beta x\pm i\sin\beta x)$ using
Euler's formula $e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta$, then
$$y=C_1e^{(\alpha+i\beta)x}+C_2e^{(\alpha-i\beta)x}=e^{\alpha x}(c_1\cos\beta x+c_2\sin\beta x)$$
with $c_1=C_1+C_2$ and $c_2=i(C_1-C_2)$, both real constants in practice.

## 3. Examples (slides 8-10, 12)

**Example 1 (slide 8).** Solve $y''+y'-6y=0$. Characteristic equation
$r^2+r-6=0\Rightarrow(r+3)(r-2)=0$, roots $r=2,-3$. Distinct real roots:
$y=C_1e^{2x}+C_2e^{-3x}$.

**Example 2 (slide 8).** Solve $3y''+y'-y=0$. $3r^2+r-1=0\Rightarrow
r=\dfrac{-1\pm\sqrt{13}}{6}$. Distinct real roots:
$y=C_1e^{\frac{-1+\sqrt{13}}{6}x}+C_2e^{\frac{-1-\sqrt{13}}{6}x}$.

**Example 3 (slide 9).** Solve $y''+4y'+4y=0$. $r^2+4r+4=(r+2)^2=0$, repeated
root $r=-2$: $y=C_1e^{-2x}+C_2xe^{-2x}$.

**Example 4 (slide 9).** Solve $y''+y'+y=0$. $r^2+r+1=0\Rightarrow
r=\dfrac{-1\pm i\sqrt3}{2}$, so $\alpha=-\frac12$, $\beta=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}$:
$y=e^{-x/2}\left(c_1\cos\frac{\sqrt3}{2}x+c_2\sin\frac{\sqrt3}{2}x\right)$.

**Example 5 (slide 10), an initial-value problem.** Solve
$y''+2y'+y=0,\ y(0)=1,\ y'(0)=3$. $r^2+2r+1=(r+1)^2=0$, repeated root $r=-1$:
$y=C_1e^{-x}+C_2xe^{-x}$. Then $y'=-C_1e^{-x}+C_2e^{-x}-C_2xe^{-x}$. From
$y(0)=1$: $C_1=1$. From $y'(0)=3$: $-C_1+C_2=3\Rightarrow C_2=4$. Solution:
$$y=e^{-x}+4xe^{-x}$$

## 4. Boundary-value problems (slides 11-12)

**Definition (slide 11).** A **boundary-value problem** asks for a solution of
a second-order equation that satisfies conditions at two different points,
e.g. $y(x_1)=y_1,\ y(x_2)=y_2$ (instead of $y(x_0)$ and $y'(x_0)$ at one
point, as in an initial-value problem).

**Remark (slide 11).** A boundary-value problem may have exactly one solution,
infinitely many, or none at all.

**Example 6 (slide 12).** Solve $y''+2y'+y=0,\ y(0)=1,\ y(1)=3$. General
solution (as in Example 5): $y=C_1e^{-x}+C_2xe^{-x}$. $y(0)=1\Rightarrow C_1=1$.
$y(1)=3\Rightarrow e^{-1}+C_2e^{-1}=3\Rightarrow C_2=3e-1$. Solution:
$$y=e^{-x}+(3e-1)xe^{-x}$$

## 5. Summary (slide 13)

For $ay''+by'+cy=0$ with characteristic equation $ar^2+br+c=0$:

| Roots of $ar^2+br+c=0$ | General solution |
|---|---|
| distinct real $r_1\ne r_2$ | $y=C_1e^{r_1x}+C_2e^{r_2x}$ |
| repeated real $r_1=r_2=r$ | $y=C_1e^{rx}+C_2xe^{rx}$ |
| complex $\alpha\pm i\beta$ | $y=e^{\alpha x}(c_1\cos\beta x+c_2\sin\beta x)$ |
